# For Everyone:

The pages listed below are where my various teaching material and musings live. This will be an ever growing and changing set, which I will attempt to organize sensibly as I go. To start, I highly recommend going to the section labelled most applicably to your situation.

# For Employers:

Thank you for having a look at my work! The [Assorted Teaching Materials page](/Teaching%20Materials/Assorted%20Teaching%20Materials) is a collection of my work from previous teaching contexts, along with links to external sites that I am affiliated with or have worked with before. Some of the material I have produced in the past is proprietary to businesses such as [Paper.co](https://paper.co) and Nelson Education, but I have tried to make available as much as possible, so that you can get a feel for how I think about the subjects I teach. All of my work is highly flexible in terms of medium, duration, learning outcome, and applicability to learning standards and competencies (I juggle at least four different national curricula when I conceptualize lessons). Most importantly, my material affords high levels of differentiation, individualized learning outcomes and adaptive assessments, as well as a variety of classroom set-ups and styles. The goings-on inside my head change as I read and experiment and, most of all, learn from my students, but [How I Think About Teaching](/Teaching%20Materials/Guides/How%20I%20Think%20About%20Teaching) can give you at least a snapshot.

# For Students:

If you are in one of my classes and have been referred here by a link from me, welcome! Feel free to peruse the sections that you find interesting and helpful. If you have any questions, you know how to reach me!

Some of the pages here are very much guides for the perplexed: opinionated writing designed to get you thinking and feeling about your learning, how you engage, and how you are best able to teach yourself. You can of course start with the subject that you are interested in, and many of you are worried about tests and homework. But I would recommend starting with [How I Recommend Learning to Learn](/Teaching%20Materials/Guides/How%20I%20Recommend%20Learning%20to%20Learn). It is a bit of a different guide to learning, based on some things I wrote in my first years of teaching in both LA and Berlin. I hope these and other words prove helpful, but, as you will hear from me in class, don’t be afraid to experiment, try things out, and write about what you did and what you learned. Your reflections on your learning adventures are just as valuable as what you learned!

# For Parents:

If you have a learner in one of my classes and have been referred here by me, welcome! If you have found your way here through other means, you are just as welcome, but you might require a couple more sentences of introduction to understand what the heck all these words are.

This is a webpage which all of my students have access to, in addition to any Content Management System (CMS) or Learning Content Management System (LCM... for some reason the S is often left out of the acronym). Here, and on such platforms, learners will be able to find worksheets and links to resources to help in their learning. My courses have a mix of more traditional structures and some which might be unfamiliar. Writing (in the form of reflections, project logs and check-ins) and reading are important parts of my classes. These aspects are not graded for content, but experience shows that students who engage in these non-technical aspects of learning tend to perform significantly better at standardized assessments, not to mention succeed more readily in the course overall.

If you have questions or concerns about my teaching methods and practice, please feel free to write me an email or fill out the contact form on the main page. Your child’s success in learning and assessment is of the utmost importance to me. We are on the same team, and your involvement in your child’s learning journey, and your dedication to helping them succeed, can greatly enhance their experience in my classes and others. Giving a learner enough space to explore and experiment, but not to flounder and flail, and enough room to make mistakes without becoming alienated from the subject, is a difficult balance to strike, whether as teacher or parent. If you are concerned that your child is not getting the attention they deserve, or that their individual learning plan needs particular attention, feel free to contact me and we can sort everything out.
