> [!note]
> These answers are short on purpose. The long version of most of them is on [How My Classes Work](/Teaching%20Materials/How%20My%20Classes%20Work), and the details for a particular module are on its page under [Current Teaching](/Current%20Teaching) and on Moodle. If your question is not here, ask me in class or by email and I will probably add it.

## Am I allowed to use Chat GPT and other LLM/AI tools?

Yes, on two conditions. Say what you used and what it did, in your planning or your readme, the same way you would cite a tutorial. And be able to explain every line you submit, because the work has to be yours in the sense that you understand it. In some modules there are specific rules in the brief, and those win. If you are unsure, ask before you submit rather than after.

## How will I be graded?

Mostly on what you make: projects, skills demonstrations and, in some modules, a college exam. My assessments do not involve a terminal grade after one attempt; there are usually several opportunities to improve. The weightings for each module are in its brief on Moodle.

## How can I improve my grade?

Engage, submit, and reflect. The more you show me what you are doing, the more easily I can see what you have learned and where you need something more, and the more flexibility I can give you. If something did not go well, come and talk to me and we will make a plan.

## Do I have to do reflections?

Yes, in the sense that they are part of nearly every class: planning at the start, reflection at the end. They are not graded for content. Students who do them do noticeably better in the course, which is the only reason I insist.

## Why are there no due dates on assignments?

Weekly work does not have hard due dates because people learn at different speeds and I would rather you did the work well than on Tuesday. Briefs, skills demonstrations and exams do have dates, set by the college, and those are on Moodle. If you are going to miss one, tell me before it passes, not after.

## What does a Check-In entail?

A short conversation, or a few written lines: what you did, what got stuck, what you are doing next. It should take five minutes. It is how I find out what is going on before it becomes a problem.

## How much do I have to write for a project update?

Enough that somebody else could pick up where you left off. Usually that is a few sentences: what changed since last time, what you tried that did not work, and what is next. A screenshot or a link helps.

## How do I know when a project is done?

When it meets the brief and you can explain every part of it. Done is not the same as perfect. If you are past the brief and still adding things, write down what you would do next and submit.

## Will there be any quizzes or tests?

There are no surprise quizzes. Some modules have a college exam, and for those we do practice exams beforehand and you will have a formula or reference sheet where the module allows it. The dates are on Moodle and on your course page.

## What are assessments?

Anything that shows me, and you, what you have learned: a set of problems, a set of problems you make up yourself, a reflection, a project, a skills demonstration, an exam. In our classes most of it is continuous rather than a single event at the end. The Assessment section of How My Classes Work says more.

## Do I have to use the online resources you recommend?

No. They are the ones I know to be good, but if something else works better for you, use it and tell me what it was, so I can recommend it to the next person. The [Resources](/Resources%20and%20Recommendations) page has the list.
