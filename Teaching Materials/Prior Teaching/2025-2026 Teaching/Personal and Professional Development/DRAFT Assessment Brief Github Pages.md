# Assessment Brief: Professional GitHub Pages Portfolio

## Personal and Professional Development 6N1949

**Type:** Skills Demonstration (15%) + Collection of Work (15%) = **30% of final grade**

**Submission Deadline:** [To be announced]

---

## Overview

Throughout this module, you have been developing a professional GitHub Pages site. This assessment asks you to submit your completed site with all required pages.

Your GitHub Pages site serves as both:

- **A skills demonstration** of your ability to plan, implement, and present professional materials

- **A collection of work** showing your reflection on personal and professional development

---

## What to Submit

### Your Live GitHub Pages Site

**Submit the URL to your completed site.**

Your site must include the following pages/sections:

Required Content:

1. **About/Bio** - Who you are and your current context

1. **Skills & Experience** - Programming languages, technical skills, relevant experience

1. **What I’m Working On** - Current projects, learning goals, or areas of focus (this shows your active development)

1. **Projects or Portfolio** - At least 2 examples of your work (can include coursework)

1. **CV/Resume Link** - Link to downloadable CV (PDF format preferred)

1. **Contact/Links** - How to reach you (LinkedIn, email, etc.)

1. **Development Process** - A page reflecting on how you built this site (see worksheet below)

**Site Requirements:**

- Professional presentation appropriate for job applications

- Clear navigation between pages/sections

- Proper use of Markdown formatting

- All links functional (especially CV download)

- No placeholder/lorem ipsum text

---

## Development Process Page

Create a page on your site called “Development Process” (or “About This Site” or similar) that addresses the following questions. **Total length: ~400 words.**

Use the worksheet provided separately to draft your responses, then add this as a page to your site.

*Note: If you prefer to keep this reflection private, you can create it as a page, download it, and then remove it from your public site before final submission—just submit the downloaded version separately.*

## Tips for Success

- **“What I’m Working On” is key** - This shows you’re actively developing, even if projects aren’t finished yet

- **Make your CV accessible** - Test the download link to ensure it works

- **Keep Development Process reflection honest** - We want to see real learning, not perfection

- **Be specific in your reflection** - Reference actual examples (what feedback, what changes, what decisions)

- **Proofread everything** - Both your site content and development process page should be error-free

- **Check all links** - Make sure your CV downloads and any project links work

---

## Academic Integrity

Your GitHub Pages site should represent your own work and experience. You may use templates or examples as inspiration, but the content must be your own. Your documentation should reflect your authentic process and learning.
