We also began to discuss how probability and calculus connect: namely that probability does not have a “sense of time” just has the “outcome” of an instantaneous “experiment”... We will use some ideas of calculus to move from probability over discrete spaces with discrete experiments to more continuous “rate of change” type problems, which will help us toward moving into higher dimensional spaces (in which we can get a machine to learn truly amazing stuff with calculus and probability).

Finally we began discussing four different probability problems, which we will discuss again tomorrow (1 and 2). If you can generate pictures for these I can upload them here! I challenge you to try to generate the images with code!

1. Triangle problem: cut an equilateral triangle in half down the middle of one side and chop off the top half (horizontal line). What is the probability that a dart hits the upper right little triangle.

1. Target Circle problem: find the probability of landing a dart in all 8 combinations of regions of three concentric circles: a radius 1 circle , a radius 2 circle , and a radius 4 circle. Can you generate a binary inclusion system for figuring out whether you have seen all the combinations?

1. Vowels and Consonant problem: Generate a list of all 5 letter words (n letter words?) with alternating vowels and consonants. What is the probability that a random 5 letter word is on your list?

1. 4x4 grid, choose n = 2 squares: what is the probability that k = 2 squares are touching? First lets say touching just means up, down, left, or right, then lets say we include diagonals, how does the probability change? (Challenge: can you also do this for n> 2 and k > 2?)
