# Week of January 5

## Research and Study Skills - Semester 2 Overview

This section outlines the second semester plan for the Research and Study Skills module, building on the foundational work from Semester 1.

---

### January: Review and Media Literacy

### Semester 1 Review

The semester begins with a review of key concepts from the first term, including frames and ideology, understanding audiences, types of sources and evaluating “credibility,” drafts and structure, resource analysis, and format and register.

### Looking at News

This unit explores how news media operates, covering the structure of articles and texts, sourcing practices, and the press as an institution. Students will complete a mini project applying these concepts, building on the framing work from earlier in the year.

---

### February: University Writing and Research

This month focuses on developing research skills that will serve students in further education and beyond.

### What We Will Explore

Students will engage with tools and strategies for academic research, culminating in a mini project. The emphasis is on applying these strategies and using research tools across different types of content and contexts.

---

### March: Academic Writing and Study Skills

### Writing with Quotes and Attribution

This unit addresses how to integrate sources, quotations, and scholarly voices into written work. A key theme is “un-learning secondary school” approaches to citation and moving toward more sophisticated academic writing practices.

### Study Skills and Time Management

The second half of March turns to practical study skills, including tools, strategies, and applications for managing time effectively. Students will consider how these approaches might work in their own lives and contexts.

---

### April: Assessment Period

April is dedicated to completing and submitting final assessments. Specific due dates will be confirmed in class.

---

*This plan is subject to adjustment based on how the semester unfolds. Questions and feedback are always welcome.*

This week was about working on drafts: it was lovely to see the progress being made on your various pieces! I could see tons of creativity and real focus on how you best represent your frame and meet the needs of your audience while accomplishing your organization's goals.

## Some General Feedback

The biggest piece of feedback I can give so far is to do yourself a favour (and myself when reading your drafts or final copies) and at the top of your draft, include the following:(Again, for yourself as much as me, this will make your reflection way easier!)

**What frame did you choose and why?** (Most of you did this so no worries there!)

**Who is your audience and why did you selected them?** (This was something I think folks were struggling with, wanting to choose more than one audience or just address “everyone”... as we have discussed in class, functional writing works best when its author has a clear understanding of their audience and their audience’s needs, motivations, interests, and more!)

**What format did you choose** ***and why?*** The why is I think the key one here: no format is easier or harder than any other, what matters is whether you think you can address an audience well with that format... in my experience, the fewer words on the actual content (like in a poster or short social media post) the harder it is to choose those words (even if you have no problem discussing why you chose them... its really easy to get stuck in your own head, and not get in the head of your audience!)

## Introductory Task:

On a new word doc please answer the following questions and go through the process below:

1. What are three surprising or useful facts that you carry around with you? If you can’t think of any how would you go about finding some? What sites or tools would you use?

1. Where did you learn these facts? If you wanted to verify them, how would you?

**Task: try to verify the facts that you have specified. What are your sources, how do you know that that source is credible or “good”? Do you find any conflicting sources?**

**Homework:** After you have answered those questions, please write a 150-250 word reflection on what was easy or difficult about this these questions and this task: what was unexpected? How did you verify your source? If you had to challenge or critique how you went about answering those questions, what would you say?
