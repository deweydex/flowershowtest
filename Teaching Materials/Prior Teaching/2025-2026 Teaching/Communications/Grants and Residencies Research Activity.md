## Overview

This activity asks you to research real funding and residency opportunities that exist for artists in Ireland and internationally. The goal is to demystify the grants landscape and help you understand what kinds of support are actually available, what they're asking for, and whether they might be right for your practice—now or in the future.

Many artists avoid applying for grants because they assume "it's not for people like me" or "I'm not experienced enough." This research will help you see the full spectrum of opportunities, from beginner-friendly to highly competitive, and understand what funders are actually looking for.

---

## Why This Matters

Grants and residencies can provide:

- **Time**: Dedicated space to develop work without the pressure of immediate sales

- **Money**: Materials budgets, living stipends, or both

- **Space**: Access to specialized equipment, studios, or locations

- **Community**: Connection with other artists, mentors, or new audiences

- **Legitimacy**: Recognition that strengthens future applications and your CV

- **Learning**: Opportunities to develop new skills or techniques

Understanding how funding works—and what the strings attached actually are—is essential for building a sustainable practice. This research helps you become strategic about which opportunities to pursue and when.

## Research Questions for Each Opportunity

For each of your three selected opportunities, answer the following questions thoroughly. These questions will help you understand not just whether you're eligible, but whether this opportunity actually serves your practice and goals.

### 1. Basic Information

- What is the name of the grant/residency/award?

- Who is the funding/hosting organization?

- What is the total value (money, in-kind support, etc.)?

- Application deadlines (if rolling, note that)

- Website/contact information

### 2. Eligibility & Experience Level

- Who is this for? (Students, emerging artists, mid-career, specific demographics?)

- What does "emerging" or "early-career" actually mean to them?

- Do you need to be a certain age?

- Do you need to be based in Ireland/EU, or can international artists apply?

- Do you need previous exhibition history? How much?

- Are there any disciplinary restrictions? (Some grants are ceramics-only, some exclude craft, etc.)

### 3. What They're Funding

- Is this for creating new work, or can it support existing projects?

- Is it for a single work or a body of work?

- Does it have to result in an exhibition? Public presentation? Publication?

- Can it be for research/development without a finished outcome?

- Is it about learning a new technique/skill?

- Is it about contributing to a group project or community initiative?

- Is teaching or public engagement required (workshops, talks, open studios)?

### 4. The Practical Details

- How long is the grant/residency period?

- Is it full-time or can you keep other work/commitments?

- If it's a residency: is accommodation provided? Meals? Travel costs?

- If it's a grant: what can the money be spent on? (Materials? Living costs? Equipment? Assistance?)

- Do you have to be in residence, or can you work from your own studio?

- What facilities/equipment are provided?

### 5. The Strings Attached (Read the Fine Print)

- Who owns the work you create during the grant period?

  - Do you retain full copyright?

  - Does the funder get reproduction rights?

  - Do they get to purchase work at a discount?

- Are you required to donate a work to their collection?

- What reporting is required? (Written reports? Documentation? Financial accounting?)

- Is there a public outcome required? (Exhibition, talk, open studio, publication?)

- What happens if you don't complete the project as proposed?

- Are there acknowledgment requirements? (How must you credit them?)

### 6. Application Requirements

- What do they want to see?

  - Written proposal? How long?

  - Portfolio of images? How many?

  - CV/resume?

  - Budget breakdown?

  - Letters of recommendation?

  - Artist statement?

- What's the selection process? (Peer review? Panel? Interview?)

- What are they actually looking for? (Read the assessment criteria carefully)

- Do they publish success rates or how many applications they typically receive?

### 7. Your Strategic Assessment

- Why does this opportunity interest you specifically?

- What would it allow you to do that you can't do now?

- Do you feel ready to apply now, or is this a future goal?

- What would you need to develop/prepare to make your application competitive?

- Are there any red flags or concerns?

- How does this align with your current practice and goals?

---

## Deliverable Format

Create a document (can be Word, PDF, or markdown) that includes:

**Introduction** (100-150 words): Brief overview of your current practice and what kinds of support would be most valuable to you right now.

**Three Opportunity Profiles** (200-500 words each): For each opportunity, address all the research questions above in a clear, organized way. Use headers to break up sections. Write this as if you're creating a resource for your future self (or other artists).

**Reflection** (200-300 words):

- What surprised you in this research?

- What did you learn about how grants and residencies actually work?

- Do any of these feel accessible to you now, or are they future goals?

- What patterns did you notice across different opportunities?

- What concerns or questions do you still have about seeking funding?

---

## Assessment Notes

This is a research and reflection exercise, not a test of your ability to find the "right" opportunities. You're assessed on:

- **Thoroughness of research**: Did you dig into the details and answer all the questions?

- **Critical thinking**: Did you read the fine print and think strategically about what these opportunities actually offer and cost?

- **Self-awareness**: Did you honestly assess where you are and what you'd need to be competitive?

- **Clarity of writing**: Is your research organized and easy to follow?

This is not assessed on:

- Finding prestigious or highly competitive grants

- Already being "ready" to apply

- Having an impressive practice history

## Tips for This Research

**Be Realistic About Timelines:** Many students discover that "emerging artist" grants actually expect 3-5 years of post-graduation exhibition history. That's not a failure on your part—it's useful information for planning.

**Watch for Hidden Costs:** Some residencies are "free" but don't cover travel or materials. Is the opportunity worth €500 in flights and €200 in clay? Maybe, maybe not.

**Read Past Recipients:** Most organizations list previous winners. Look them up. What were their practices like? Does your work fit that pattern, or would you be an outlier?

**Beware of Vanity Opportunities:** If you're paying more than €100 to apply, or if the residency charges you significant fees for the "privilege" of working there, it's worth questioning whether this is really support or just a business exploiting artists.

**Geographic Realities:** That amazing residency in rural Japan sounds incredible, but if you have caring responsibilities, chronic illness, or can't afford the flight... it's not really an opportunity for you right now. That's okay. There are other paths.

**Start Local:** Irish artists often overlook local council grants because they seem "small" (€500-€2000), but they're often less competitive and the application is simpler. Local funding can be a stepping stone to national grants.

## Additional Resources

**Reading the Arts Council:** The Arts Council site is dense. Start with "Awards" or "Funding" and filter by artform. Don't skip the eligibility criteria—it's there for a reason.

**VAI (Visual Artists Ireland):** VAI regularly posts grant opportunities and also offers advice on applications. If you're not signed up for their newsletter, do it.

**Ask Working Artists:** The best information often comes from artists who've actually been through the process. If you know someone who's held a grant or residency, ask them about the experience—especially the parts that weren't in the official description.

**Timeline Awareness:** Many major grants have annual deadlines (often Jan-March). If you find something perfect but the deadline was last month, save it for next year and start preparing now.
