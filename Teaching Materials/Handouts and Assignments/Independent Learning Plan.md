During most classes with me, each of us will generally be learning at our own pace, punctuated by group practice or discussion. In order for that to work, and for all of us to better learn how to set goals and take responsibility of our own learning, occasionally we will each create an *Independent Learning Plan.* This can be done using a template (check your course page for a link if I have mentioned one!) or just answering the following questions on a physical or digital piece of paper.

Key questions:

1. What will you accomplish over what period of time? It can help to think of a STAR Goal framework:

- **S**PECIFIC – Define what you want to achieve. This usually is in the form of a certain number of exercises on a specific topic (For example, 20 problems about factoring polynomials, or 8 exercises involving python for-loops.)

- **T**IMELY – When do you want to achieve the goal? (A specific date/class-period, try to give yourself enough time to get things done, but not too much time that it feels abstract and not urgent.)

- **A**CTION ORIENTED – How will you achieve the goal? (What specific steps will you take!)

- **R**EALISTIC – Is the goal attainable? What might stand in your way?

If you need to change the date that you estimated, or run into other issues, that is totally fine, these are guidelines and goals, not written in stone.

If you would like problems or questions from other resources or from me (I always am up for giving nice little problems) just let me know! :)

2. How you will accomplish your goal? This is usually about discussing what resources you will use as well as what amount of time you will spend. Since part of your goal might involve producing something (like a computer program that generates random numbers) you can outline the steps you would take to get that done.  What specific actions will you take? Where will you find your exercises? What file structure will you use for your project? Is there a framework or step-by-step process you will use? Are there things you will check your program for? Will you make sure you wrote out steps as evidence?

3. What challenges or hurdles might stand in your way? What might make accomplishing your goal difficult? Most importantly: what will you do to overcome those challenges?

Sometimes the biggest challenges we face are physical, feeling tired, or unfocused. Sometimes it is not clear what the next steps should be or we might be struggling with our confidence level. This section is not about being overly optimistic or pessimistic, but for outlining the real and present issues we might face when we go about a given task.

You are welcome (encouraged even!) to send me your plans (or show me in person) and discuss them with me in class. If you are looking for resources or confused about how to take the next step, or even just unsure about what a useful and realistic goal is, I am here, and feel free to ask a friend about their plan or include them in it as a source of feedback or to check your knowledge.

As always, questions and comments and suggestions are welcome! If some part of the above isn't clear or doesn't make sense, maybe I can fix it to make it better or easier! Good luck!
